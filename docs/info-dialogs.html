Prose source: www/section-docs.json · docs.massBtn

A mass flock is an unusually large gathering of one kind of bird. This menu shows reported flocks that meet the app's size and evidence rules, including flocks that do not appear in Bird Gen.

Sources, calculations and limitations

All qualifying high-count flocks in the active region, including those without a Bird Gen news qualifier.

Data it reads

  • the same current-month regional High Count page, verified public-hotspot directory and retained Mass evidence used by Bird Gen

How it is calculated

  • Reuse Mass Flock count, recency, scope, independent-visit and public-location qualification. The current High Count source explicitly permits single-observation evidence.
  • Do not apply Bird Gen's personal proximity/new-arrival admission gate. Each flock states whether it currently earns Bird Gen admission.
  • Reuse retained evidence and source freshness. Failed, partial, retained and verified-empty results remain distinct. Excluded malformed rows are counted and logged with bounded privacy-redacted details.
  • Bird and public-place links open the existing Stakeout pages; dates retain their supporting checklist links.

What it cannot tell you

  • Current-month high counts are not an exhaustive daily observation inventory. Unresolved or private locations are not promoted to public destinations.
  • More inclusive browsing does not broaden Bird Gen notification eligibility.

When birders visit for one rare bird, they sometimes discover other unusual birds nearby. This is called the Patagonia Picnic Table Effect. This menu shows reported gatherings that match that pattern; the reports do not prove why the other birds were found.

Sources, calculations and limitations

A dedicated feed of all qualified Patagonia Picnic Table Effect rarity gatherings.

Data it reads

  • the existing active-region/county RBA sources, matched public-hotspot identities and retained PPTE evidence

How it is calculated

  • Reuse the exact PPTE qualification: a celebrity-style rarity anchor followed by another rarity, with separate checklist and reporting-name corroboration and per-bird recency.
  • Show qualified events independently of Bird Gen's category selection or ranking; do not relax the event definition to manufacture a longer feed.

Section calculation disclosure🔔 Bird GenTalk and news from current bird reports, migration evidence, personal discoveries and public patches. Category explanations below describe why each alert qualifies.

Surface: App section and archived-report section · App anchor: surgeBtn

Context: section results and any live warning vary by report and fetched data. This entry shows authored prose only.

Prose source: www/section-docs.json · docs.surgeBtn

Bird Gen brings together birding news from several kinds of reports. Use it to spot noteworthy birds, busy patches, migration news and discoveries relevant to your selected area.

Sources, calculations and limitations

Talk and news from current bird reports, migration evidence, personal discoveries and public patches. Category explanations below describe why each alert qualifies.

Data it reads

  • the region's ABA Code 3+ snapshot, refreshed lazily after the initial render frame and priority observation requests are queued
  • county recent + notable feeds
  • the region's Top 100 board
  • hotspot activity and the complete destination projection in the same feeds

How it is calculated

  • Personal status uses the exact selected Current year or All time list after duplicate species and reasons are merged. Only complete history or an explicit complete zero proves absence. Incomplete positives remain known; unknown history is neutral, not unseen. Species-blind HOTSPOT alerts have no personal status.
  • Custom reports resolve the existing county acquisition scope before planning hotspot checklist feeds and cache keys. Independent observation results can paint while scope loads. Failed scope resolution is explicit, not not-applicable or known-empty, and county hydration does not cancel independent Mass/Favorite work.
  • Bird Gen renders its shell or retained results before starting acquisition. After priority observation requests are queued, the other sources run independently; one slow Favorite no longer hides completed leaderboard or hotspot results. Mega and BirdCast are owned background sources, not prerequisites for rendering.
  • Favorite patches publish eligible birds one patch at a time. Mass Flock publishes checked species while later corroboration requests run; private-location rows stay withheld until the existing public-location checks finish. Existing evidence thresholds, candidate caps and final ordering are unchanged.
  • Still-relevant last-good results survive reopen and refresh-age expiry, with an explicit retained timestamp and source state. Observation dates, current seen status, Home, radius and scope still govern eligibility. A failed refresh does not clear successful cached evidence; a successful response replaces its matching source rather than accumulating obsolete alerts forever. Complete retained chase evidence can supply TOP PATCH alerts; partial and failed destination views cannot.
  • Performance samples distinguish first content, primary observation readiness and owned source settlement. A quiet DOM does not finish a pending source. Heading and pull refresh start a new sample unless they join an existing update. Viewport-lazy photos are explicitly excluded from source-complete timing.
  • Places that read as private - a named residence, a street address, bare coordinates - are never offered as somewhere to drive. Word, glyph, underline and distinct border style carry meaning without relying on colour.

What it cannot tell you

  • A report whose date cannot be read is dropped from a freshness-gated source rather than assumed fresh.
  • The baseline is measured from the data actually fetched (7 days), capped at the configured window, so a short feed cannot inflate every ratio.

A twitch is a trip to look for a particular reported bird. This menu shows weather, daylight and available tide information to help you decide when to go.

Sources, calculations and limitations

Whether today's weather and tide favour going out, for the region's home coordinate.

Data it reads

  • NOAA forecast (US only)
  • NOAA tides for the region's station

How it is calculated

  • Sunrise, sunset and civil twilight are computed for the home coordinate, in the region's local time.
  • Tides are listed one row per WINDOW, not per turning point, because the fact that decides whether you leave the house is when the water is coming IN — reconstructing that from two adjacent turning points is work the reader should not do.

  • Tides need a NOAA station id; inland regions leave it blank and get no tide table.

Prose source: www/section-docs.json · docs.rankBtn

eBird's Top 100 lists the leading birders in an area by species or checklists. This menu shows those rankings and your position when your profile is available.

Sources, calculations and limitations

Your standing on the region's eBird Top 100, plus the board itself.

Data it reads

  • ebird.org/top100 for the region (public HTML, no API key)

How it is calculated

  • Species and Checklists are separately fetched official boards for one exact geography and selected Current year or All time period. Returned metric, period and geography must match.
  • The full-width Species / Checklists control selects the official metric-owned rank, count, order, personal standing and history without another read. Never infer checklist rank from species counts.
  • Each toggle shows only participants returned by that metric's own board, with no cross-board row synthesis or count enrichment. One failed board leaves the other usable with partial coverage; two failures are not a verified empty board.
  • "of N eBirders" comes from a separate endpoint that needs eBird's own web token, scraped from the site bundle.
  • The app records one successful owner-rank snapshot per day, separated by region, period, metric, profile and identity revision because eBird cannot re-serve a past board.
  • Season best is the lowest valid rank in those on-device snapshots. If that rank was reached more than once, the card names the first date it was reached.
  • The compact target tables remain at the top on both toggles and are explicitly Species targets, using only the Species source and a verified eBird profile ID. They show five returned birders above and below you, same-board gaps, the next-higher tie/exceed target, an available Top 100 cutoff and how many higher returned rows five more species would exceed. Missing identity offers Verify my eBird profile; missing or gapped coverage remains unavailable.
  • Species retains up to 500 returned rows from the existing 500-result request, reusing its daily account-owned cache with Leaderboard Ticks and joining duplicate in-flight reads. The main Top 100 board stays limited to its existing presentation.
  • Latest-bird images are 56px before display scaling and sit immediately left of the bird name, not in the rank column.

What it cannot tell you

  • eBird runs a proof-of-work bot filter on some region pages. When the token cannot be resolved the field size is omitted rather than failing the section — so a missing "of N" means degraded, not zero.
  • Each published day is a dated snapshot; eBird cannot re-serve yesterday's board.
  • Nearby targets assume unchanged competitor totals and do not guarantee future ranks. Missing profile identity, gaps around the owner, stale reads and response boundaries are stated explicitly. A separately returned owner summary does not establish neighbor coverage. No larger or growing-prefix request is made for deep ranks.
Section calculation disclosure💪 Pro patchesWhere the hundred busiest local birders actually go, ranked by how disproportionately they choose a place rather than by how many checklists it holds.

Surface: App section and archived-report section · App anchor: patchBtn

Context: section results and any live warning vary by report and fetched data. This entry shows authored prose only.

Prose source: www/section-docs.json · docs.patchBtn

A patch is a place people go birding. This menu highlights places the area's busiest birders choose unusually often, rather than simply the places with the most checklists.

Sources, calculations and limitations

Where the hundred busiest local birders actually go, ranked by how disproportionately they choose a place rather than by how many checklists it holds.

Data it reads

  • data/wa/choice-patches.json, built from 730 harvested county-days (product/lists/{region}/{y}/{m}/{d})
  • eBird's ref/hotspot index, to tell a hotspot from a personal pin
  • ebird.org/top100 for the statewide board rank annotation only

How it is calculated

  • A place's score is a LIFT: the share of that birder's checklists filed there, over the share of everyone's checklists filed there. 5x means they choose it five times as often as the counties as a whole do.
  • Both shares are evidence-weighted — a 95% Wilson lower bound on the birder's share over a Wilson upper bound on the population's — so a place visited once cannot tie a place visited two hundred times.
  • Personal pins are excluded and only eBird hotspots are ranked: pins are 92% of places but under half the effort, and a birder is usually their sole visitor, which is what made them dominate a raw ranking.
  • The population is the top 100 by LOCAL EFFORT in the report's counties, not the statewide species board. The default Leaderboard filter shows birders ranked on any of those county boards or the statewide board; All reveals the full local-effort population. Each row lists every available standing with its scope, such as Washington #89 · King #12 · Snohomish #24. The left marker uses the statewide rank when present, otherwise the best county rank; N/R explicitly means no listed rank. It is never the row's position in the lift-sorted list.

What it cannot tell you

  • Coverage is part of the answer: this sees only the checklists eBird publishes, so a birder who keeps their lists private looks like they have no patch. Those birders are NAMED rather than ranked low.
  • Two counties (King, Snohomish) over 365 days. A birder who birds elsewhere in the state will not appear.
  • The join key is a display NAME — eBird's day feed carries no user id — so two birders sharing a name would merge.

These are places with reported birds you still need for your selected personal list. This menu ranks eligible outings by how many needed birds they could add, using your chase settings and the available reports.

Sources, calculations and limitations

The places holding the most unseen birds, ranked by how many you would gain.

  • geo feeds around home
  • in Hawaii only, one bounded 30-day public-hotspot feed when fewer than four fresh choices remain

Section calculation disclosure🚗 Day trip patchesTarget-rich excursions split into distinct round-trip travel bands.

Surface: App section and archived-report section · App anchor: excBtn

Context: section results and any live warning vary by report and fetched data. This entry shows authored prose only.

Prose source: www/section-docs.json · docs.excBtn

A day-trip patch is a possible longer outing to look for birds you need. This menu groups target-rich destinations by round-trip travel band so you can compare outings of different lengths.

Sources, calculations and limitations

Target-rich excursions split into distinct round-trip travel bands.

Data it reads

  • the same clustered unseen reports as Today’s patches
  • eBird’s county list and county bounding boxes for the active state
  • the shared travel-zone route-friction model
  • in Hawaii only, the same bounded 30-day public-hotspot feed used by the other patch boards when fresh evidence is sparse

How it is calculated

  • The first open loads county names and bounds from eBird and caches each valid result permanently. A failed or malformed county is not cached and is retried on refresh.
  • Under 3h, 3–5h, 5–8h, and 8h+ are distinct estimated round-trip bands. A hotspot appears in only one choice.
  • County acquisition is derived from Home and the selected band’s upper bound. The open-ended 8h+ choice uses every hotspot-represented county in the active state or region. Moving Home recomputes the bounded selections from the same cached metadata.
  • Bounding boxes decide only which feeds are worth buying. Each actual hotspot is classified from its own coordinates using straight mileage plus route friction.
  • An explicitly offshore, pelagic, moving-ferry, open-water, or strait location is a special trip even when its straight-line distance falls inside Today’s boundary, so it routes here instead of being presented as drivable.
  • The board starts beyond the exact adaptive boundary Today’s patches used, so one hotspot cannot appear in both.
  • Fresh rows remain first. In Hawaii, a sparse board reuses the county-scoped 30-day cache, deduplicates the same place, and visibly age-labels each older-evidence card.
  • Rarities count ×3, needs-verification keeps its reduced weight, and distance decay makes a farther site earn the drive.

What it cannot tell you

  • A county box can include inaccessible terrain near Home, so scope deliberately overfetches. It never assigns a travel band to the whole county.
  • The county scope is selected from Home. Here and Find may rerank loaded cards, but they do not silently acquire a different state-sized data set.
  • The first cold catalog is expensive, but it is paid only when Day trip patches is opened and never again for valid county bounds.
  • The 30-day eBird endpoint is species-collapsed rather than a complete hotspot history, so the app must not invent a fuller result than the evidence supports.
  • A forced refresh keeps the matching tier snapshot and visible cards until live county feeds provide a replacement; unavailable feeds are labelled as cached rather than rendered as empty.

Nearby hotspots, with an Unseen view for the closest places holding birds you still need.

Data it reads

  • Pick the anchor — home, your current location, or a place you search for. Home is the default, which is what the Markdown report uses.

  • The map contains exactly the places currently visible in the list; switching All/Unseen retains deliberately expanded cards.
Section calculation disclosure🦉 Stakeout BirdsSearch for any bird or spuh, then continue from a spuh candidate into its recent places and checklists.

Surface: App-only section · App anchor: spLookupBtn

Context: section results and any live warning vary by report and fetched data. This entry shows authored prose only.

Prose source: www/section-docs.json · docs.spLookupBtn

A stakeout starts with a bird you want to find. Search for a bird or broader identification group, then explore its reported places and supporting checklists.

Sources, calculations and limitations

Search for any bird or spuh, then continue from a spuh candidate into its recent places and checklists.

Data it reads

  • eBird product/spplist for the active region and, only after an empty match, each parent region in order
  • data/obs/{region}/recent/{code}, the per-species observation feed
  • ref/taxonomy/ebird, fetched directly with your key and cached as a compact on-device model for the bird hierarchy
  • when opened from Mega rarities only, that current in-memory Mega view's raw reports, state/ABA-wide promises and evidence
  • the search field and Go action share the primary row; Close and Codes remain available in the secondary row

How it is calculated

  • Typed text is matched against species common names, scientific names and codes. The active region is searched first; only if it has no match does the search climb through its parent regions, stopping at the first useful level. Go submits the search; Close clears the selected bird; Codes opens the browsable code list.
  • A parent-region result stays under an Outside {active region} heading and each row says it is not on the active regional list. Opening it still asks the original report for sightings, so a taxonomy match can never masquerade as a local observation.
  • A query ending in sp or sp. is treated as exact spuh intent before any species-list request; punctuation variants are equivalent. An exact spuh opens its bird hierarchy immediately, while several spuh matches stay as choices.
  • For ordinary text, active-region bird matches paint first while matching spuh labels load independently into the same result. Parent-region species indexes are consulted only when the active region has no bird match; full-taxonomy birds remain under an explicit Outside {active region} boundary.
  • One active-region match goes straight to the bird rather than showing a menu of one; fallback matches keep their visible scope boundary even when there is only one.
  • The full taxonomy is compacted on the device to concrete species, spuh labels and reportAs aliases, then cached in IndexedDB for 30 days. No eBird taxonomy bundle ships in the public repository.
  • Each spuh resolves to its exact published candidate-species set. Labels with identical sets share one coverage class, and minimal subset/superset relations form a DAG because real groups overlap.
  • A spuh is treated as a bird-hierarchy entry, not a separate feature. Its result uses the same medium SpeciesCards shape as a selected bird, without a separate enclosing border: a uniformly random candidate reported in the current 30-day regional snapshot supplies the clearly labelled representative image, falling back to another active regional candidate only when needed. The searched spuh is the large title, and its count and taxonomy identifiers sit on the card's metadata line.
  • Below the TAXONOMIC HIERARCHY heading, Condensed runs the verified backbone inline with the same › chevron as candidate cards between ancestry levels. Familiar spuh wording for which eBird publishes no separate bird set is regular italic with a baseline * and a small bold backslash announced as "or"; every compact taxonomy chip is an in-app Spuh Finder button with no direct eBird species href, and real spuh labels receive no invented count.
  • Detailed replaces the inline row with numbered Class, Order, Family and Genus steps. Every backbone step names its verified taxon and exact published-set count; starred spuh wording stays at the matching level without being drawn as a parent or child. Switching views preserves the representative image and the same SpeciesCards.small candidate list.
  • The hierarchy information button explains the starred notation, preserves the selected spuh's generated definition, and keeps taxonomy and example-image source boundaries out of the primary result.
  • Candidate birds are split into Recently reported and Other regional candidates. The regional feed proves only recent presence, so rows say RECENT or REGIONAL LIST and never invent per-species report counts or a most-reported ranking.
  • Selecting a candidate replaces the search field with that bird's name and removes the now-redundant spuh result before painting the bird's normal map, recent-hotspot evidence and bird hierarchy. Clicking the source-spuh chip in the concise hierarchy reopens the candidate result when a different bird is needed.
  • Observations are grouped to one shared HotspotCards.small row per location, keeping that place's most recent checklist, count and evidence.
  • Reachable shows only physically reachable places: inside the chase radius, in the report's target counties, public, and not pelagic, offshore, open-water or a named ocean basin. It sorts those places newest first. Distance sorts every regional place from nearest to farthest, including evidence hidden from Reachable. The loaded sightings summary names the active view inside the selected bird card after its selected personal-list state, including unknown history.
  • The first five places paint immediately. Show more places appends the next batch into the same list without refetching, nesting a second list or accepting a stale control from an older species lookup.
  • In the flat Recent Checklists view, a row's printed number matches its public map pin, not its checklist ordinal. Multiple checklists at the same place share a number. Private places or missing coordinates say No public pin; they never receive an invented map location. Ten-row expansion, date/distance sorting, radius filtering and Group switches update rows and pins together.
  • Viewport-near public hotspots automatically check up to six recent checklist summaries for this bird, one place at a time through shared background pacing. Known matching reports appear without a tap, with zero/one/multiple additional-match counts and the checked budget. Private locations are excluded; failed reads remain explicit and retryable, never absence. Cached successful evidence is reused and obsolete navigation cannot append.
  • A Mega-launched lookup still renders exactly one medium species card. Mega evidence is prepended inside that card while the ordinary place list, map, date/distance/iconic controls, arrival note and unwatched-place lane continue below it.
  • An honestly empty active-region sightings result keeps the Mega evidence visible rather than replacing it with an empty-state message.
  • Back returns to the source Mega list only if its report/region/SID/scope/generation key is still current; otherwise it returns to Contents.
  • Looks back 30 days, wider than the chase feeds, because a lookup is asked about birds that may not be present today.
  • The selected bird card and sightings list paint independently from taxonomy. A transport failure before any HTTP response receives one automatic sightings retry; two failures retain the bird card with a named Retry sightings control, while HTTP errors are not mistaken for transport failures. Before the map, one plain sentence shows the bird hierarchy as compact MEGA-sized clickable chips: right-arrow icons (→) separate levels, the word 'or' joins same-rank spuh siblings, and the selected bird ends the sentence. Coverage-limited siblings retain a warning glyph and dashed chip border.
  • The underlined Show Hierarchy Details text link below that sentence keeps a 44px tap target and expands the full class, order, family, genus and species alignment, coverage explanation, narrower groups, complete candidate set and comparison tool without making the default lookup a full screen of taxonomy.
  • Compare possible birds finds the minimal published spuh set containing every selection. Because spuh sets overlap, it can return more than one equally narrow answer rather than forcing a false single-parent tree.

What it cannot tell you

  • App-only: a Markdown report cannot take a bird/spuh query or candidate selection, and printing every bird's places and hierarchy paths for a region would be a phone book, not a section.
  • Absence is an answer, not an error — no rows means it is not being reported right now, which is exactly what you asked.
  • The name lookup can identify a bird outside the region, but the sightings answer remains scoped to the active report. A fallback match may therefore lead to the honest answer that there are no local reports in the active window.
  • The per-species feed returns only the most recent report at each place. One row per place is the endpoint's limit, not the bird's full history; repeated visits at the same place may be omitted.
  • Mega context is transient and exact-code only. Ordinary search and generic species links clear it, so rarity evidence cannot attach to a different bird.
  • The spuh answer proves taxonomy containment, not that the field identification is correct. It is safe only if every plausible species was selected.
  • Seventy-nine labels are shown with a dashed Coverage limit warning and are never selected automatically: 73 parenthetical glosses whose narrower membership is not published (58 share coverage with another spuh and 15 are lone labels), five named subfamilies, and the mixed Buteo/eagle group.
Section calculation disclosure🏜️ Stakeout PatchesChoose one hotspot and see its map, visit pattern, birds, recent checklists, and regular birders.

Surface: App-only section · App anchor: stakeHsBtn

Context: section results and any live warning vary by report and fetched data. This entry shows authored prose only.

Prose source: www/section-docs.json · docs.stakeHsBtn

This view helps you get to know one birding hotspot. Choose a place to explore its map, reported birds, visit patterns, recent checklists and regular birders.

Sources, calculations and limitations

Choose one hotspot and see its map, visit pattern, birds, recent checklists, and regular birders.

Data it reads

  • eBird ref/hotspot/{region} for the name search and nearest-hotspot lookup, cached
  • the device's current location when Here is selected
  • eBird product/lists/{locId}, one call, which carries recent checklist headers and observers
  • The existing rolling 30-day full hotspot observation response; one rate-limited checklist/view read per unique missing ID, reusing verified cached evidence

How it is calculated

  • The search field and Go action share the primary row. Here lists the nearest public hotspots from the cached regional directory; Close clears the query, choices and selected hotspot and rejects any late result from that cleared lookup.
  • The heading refresh is unavailable until a hotspot is open. It then reloads that same selected place and bypasses the session memo for both the live checklist headers and recent-observation feed.
  • The PATTERN leads, not today rows. How many checklists, across how many days, and what a visit is typically worth are true all day - unlike what was seen today, which is empty every morning by eBird own design.
  • The selected place leads with a map and explicit Open in Maps, Open eBird hotspot, QR, and add/remove favorite actions. Favorite changes use the same on-device favorite-hotspot list as Favorite patches.
  • MEASURED 2026-08-22 at 10:10: three target species returned ZERO reports from that day across the whole state. eBird embargoes checklists for an hour (its hotspot page says so) and people submit hours after the walk.
  • WHO BIRDS HERE is counted from the same single call, because product/lists carries userDisplayName. The patch-regular list is a different and more useful thing than the county top 100: the person to ask about a place is whoever actually birds it. Cedar River mouth reads Eric Hope 30.

Sources, calculations and limitations

The megararities that are DUE here — rare enough to be a record, and not found yet this year. Scope it to your county for targets you might actually break.

Data it reads

  • a bundled list computed offline from GBIF, refreshed on app release
  • your region, and which scope you picked — the whole state, or one county
  • today's month, to rank by what is plausible NOW

How it is calculated

  • Counts DISTINCT YEARS a species has been recorded here, never observations — one twitched bird inflates a row count 12-17x, so Terek Sandpiper's 15 Washington 'records' are one bird over two days.
  • Uses a 10-year window, because eBird's growth means an all-time count compares eras that are not comparable. Measured: this roughly triples the hit rate and halves the list. Washington's records committee reached the same rule independently — its review list is 'birds with 20 or fewer records in the previous 10-year period'.
  • Adds evidence from ecologically similar regions, which is the only way a bird with NO local history can be predicted at all. The Terek found at Stanwood in July 2026 had one prior precedent: Sooke, BC, the same fortnight in 1987.
  • At COUNTY scope the comparison region is the parent state, because a vagrant loose in Washington is loose in Washington. Measured: of the 132 species recorded in Washington but never in King County, the state reaches all 132 in one query while the three most similar counties reach 40.
  • Ranks by the odds for THIS MONTH, not by rarity. A 4% annual chance concentrated into one fortnight is a chase decision; spread across a year it is trivia.
  • Excludes escaped cagebirds by family, plus an explicit list of collection birds that families cannot express — Black Swan is a waterfowl, Golden Pheasant a phasianid, so excluding those families would delete Emperor Goose and Sooty Grouse with them.
  • The exclusions carry a per-region exemption, because countability is regional: Common Waxbill and Rose-ringed Parakeet are established and countable in Hawaii while being escapees on the mainland.

What it cannot tell you

  • It is a WATCHLIST, not a forecast. At state scope roughly one in six listed birds turns up within two years. A county record is a lower bar, which is why the county list is longer — no separate hit rate has been measured for it.
  • It cannot see a bird nobody has ever reported near you. A first record with no precedent anywhere in the region is exactly the case no history can predict.
  • Hawaii has no ecological peer in the ABA area, so its list can only contain birds already recorded there — a Hawaiian vagrant arrives off the Pacific, and no mainland state predicts it.
  • The list is bundled and only changes when the app updates, because the model needs decades of data the phone does not have. Only counties the reports cover are bundled.
  • A species rare next door because it is endangered and local, rather than because it wanders, can still slip in.
  • The escapee list is hand-authored, because no machine-readable source survived measurement — so a collection bird nobody has listed yet can still appear. African Collared-Dove does, for King County.

Bird Gen combines different kinds of birding news, each identified by a category. This explanation describes what each category means and why an item can appear in the feed.

Current feed: 3 active alerts. Mega snapshot from the ABA alert, 3h ago.

Alert-type key

  • 🚨 TWITCH — New unseen rarities
    An unseen, non-WATCH rarity newly appearing today inside the saved chase area after a successful baseline. Explicit Refresh can also recover a never-alerted opportunity with a usable in-range report in the past 24 hours, even when feed history already knows it or includes earlier reports; recovery is not proof of a new arrival. Initial baselines stay silent and previously alerted species are not re-announced. Verified unseen membership, review, source coverage and chase-distance checks still apply. Counts are known independent reports at the displayed walkable locality, not individual birds or an exhaustive total. Opens Twitches.
  • 🦤 MEGA — Mega rarities
    ABA Code 3+ rarities with qualifying new-locality or nearby-unseen evidence.
  • 🌙 MIGRATION — Migration news
    County migration forecasts, heavy overnight movement, observed arrivals or expected departures. Expected movement is not a confirmed sighting.
  • 🪶 MASS FLOCK — Mass flocks
    Large recent counts at verified public hotspots, qualifying as nearby unseen news or an evidence-backed new report.
  • 🎯 CELEBRITY — Celebrity birds
    Locally notable birds with multiple qualifying sightings within chase distance, whether already seen or not.
  • 🐦 CROWD — Species drawing a crowd
    Non-rarities attracting unusually many distinct observers at one place, relative to that species and place.
  • 🏆 CASCADE — Leaderboard cascades
    At least five distinct Top 100 birders added the same species in the past seven calendar days, with the latest addition within three days. Distant destinations need public sighting evidence within three days; leaderboard interest does not prove independent reporting parties.
  • ★ FAVORITE PATCH — Favorite Patch birds
    Fresh birds missing from your selected personal list, reported at one of your saved patches.
  • 📍 BUSY PATCH — Unusually busy patches
    At least five independent parties in 36 hours and at least four times the place’s own historical daily baseline. No lower-multiplier crowd exception; without a baseline there is no alert. The multiplier compares recent parties with historical observers per day, not equal-length windows. This signal is species-blind.
  • 🥇 TOP PATCH — High-yield top patches
    The highest-yield destinations from the complete current chase view, compared with that view’s median patch score.
  • 📅 FOY — First-of-year reports
    Newly learned first regional reports this year. These can describe earlier dates and do not establish current presence.
  • PATAGONIA — Patagonia picnic table effect
    A celebrity-style rarity plus a subsequently reported rarity at the same actual site, not merely two rare birds together. The attracting bird needs the Celebrity threshold of four separate recent sightings; shared same-minute visits count once across site aliases. Each bird needs a latest report-date age of at most three calendar days, plus two checklists and two reporting names on the same qualification day. The anchor must appear on an earlier captured date; this does not prove first discovery or causation. Mega status is not required. Qualifying PPTE events are ordered nearest first, with unknown distances last; farther events remain visible. Bird and place links have no underline. Bird names open Stakeout bird; the place opens the exact public patch or focused Twitches evidence. Small print gives the qualification date, known straight-line distance from the selected origin and qualification-day report counts, not individual birds. Unknown distance is omitted. Only known inside-radius events mention the chase area.
  • ! SENSITIVE — Sensitive species this month
    Public regional species membership for the stated calendar month. Exact observation dates and locations are withheld by eBird. This is not a located chase opportunity.

One feed sorts every active alert by newest observation or event date/time first, including newly loaded sources. Category priority and stable bird/place/checklist identity break equal-time ties. Missing or invalid dates sort last; a date without a time is labelled honestly. Migration forecasts use their validity date, not the time you refreshed.

There is no feed-wide Unseen filter. Favorite Patch eligibility is personal; flock and place news remain useful even for species already seen.

If one species qualifies more than once, it appears once under its strongest category and keeps every qualifying reason on that row in accessible category metadata. The visible category headline explains the strongest category. Place-specific favorite and flock stories remain separate.

Select a category/count tag to show only that category. The selected tag has a checkmark and emphasized border; select it again to show all alerts. The filter survives incoming results but resets whenever Bird Gen opens or refreshes. It is not saved, and filtering makes no network requests. Applicable categories remain visible while loading, with an hourglass or their known retained count. Failed and incomplete categories keep a separate retry button; filtering and retry are different actions. A healthy zero disappears only after its sources finish. Overlapping FOY reasons do not add a primary-category card.

Bird headlines read species name at place, with no inline RARE or MEGA tag. The category badge and bold explanation sit below the headline. Four-letter code, xN and linked compact date with checklist ID come first in the extra evidence below, using the same small-print styling; existing evidence and caveats follow. PPTE keeps its event-specific evidence. The alert icon and relative age remain at the right. The whole headline opens Stakeout bird or Stakeout hotspot; this news feed does not expand into report, species or checklist lists.
